ECommerce Sales Performance and Customer Purchasing Behavior¶

Abstract¶

This project examines ecommerce sales performance and customer purchasing behavior using Python and the UCI Online Retail dataset. The analysis explores monthly revenue trends, top selling products, geographic sales patterns, customer spending, and the potential impact of returns and cancellations. Using pandas, NumPy, and Matplotlib, the dataset was cleaned, summarized, and visualized to identify patterns that could support business decision-making.

Introduction¶

Ecommerce businesses generate large amounts of transactional data that can provide insights into customer behavior and sales performance. Analyzing this information can help businesses identify important products, understand seasonal purchasing trends, and evaluate customer spending patterns.

The primary research question is:

How do product performance, customer purchasing patterns, and seasonal trends contribute to overall ecommerce revenue?

The dataset comes from the UCI Machine Learning Repository and contains 541,909 transaction records from December 2010 through December 2011.

Dataset Source: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/352/online+retail

Data Cleaning¶

Before analyzing sales performance, I examined the dataset for missing values, duplicate records, negative quantities, and invalid prices. Since negative quantities may represent returns or cancellations, I will create a separate dataset containing positive sales transactions for the revenue analysis while preserving the original dataset.

In [18]:
# create a copy of the original dataset
sales = df.copy()

# Keep positive sales transactions
sales = sales[
    (sales['Quantity'] > 0) &
    (sales['UnitPrice'] > 0) &
    (~sales['InvoiceNo'].astype(str).str.startswith('C'))
].copy()

# Remove exact duplicate rows
sales = sales.drop_duplicates()

# Calculate revenue for each transaction line
sales['Revenue'] = sales['Quantity'] * sales['UnitPrice']

# Display results

Cancellations and Returns¶

This analysis examines transactions with negative quantities to estimate the value of returned or canceled merchandise. These transactions are analyzed separately from positive sales to understand their potential effect on overall revenue.

In [24]:
# Identify negative quantity transactions
returns = df[
    (df['Quantity'] < 0) &
    (df['UnitPrice'] > 0)
].copy()

# Calculate value of returned or canceled items
returns['Return_Value'] = (
    returns['Quantity'].abs() * returns['UnitPrice']
)

# Summary
print("Return/Cancellation Records:", len(returns))
print("Total Return/Cancellation Value: £{:,.2f}".format(

Interpretation¶

The analysis identified 9,288 negativequantity transaction records with positive unit prices, representing approximately £896,812 in potential returns or cancellations.

Compared with £10.64 million in positive sales revenue, this amount represents approximately 8.4%. Subtracting the negative transaction value produces a preliminary adjusted revenue estimate of £9.75 million.

These findings suggest that returns and cancellations may have a noticeable impact on overall sales performance. However, the adjusted revenue should be interpreted cautiously because some negative transactions may represent accounting adjustments rather than actual product returns.

Conclusion¶

This project explored ecommerce sales performance and customer purchasing behavior using Python and the UCI Online Retail dataset. After cleaning the original 541,909 transaction records, 524,878 positive sales records were retained for analysis, generating approximately £10.64 million in positive sales revenue.

The monthly revenue analysis showed that sales increased substantially toward the end of 2011, with November generating the highest revenue at approximately £1.50 million. Product analysis identified REGENCY CAKESTAND 3 TIER and PAPER CRAFT, LITTLE BIRDIE as two of the highest revenue merchandise items. Geographic analysis showed that the United Kingdom accounted for approximately 84.6% of positive sales revenue.

Customer analysis revealed considerable differences in purchasing behavior. The median customer spent £668.57, while average spending was £2,048.69, suggesting that higher spending customers influence overall revenue. Additionally, negative quantity transactions with positive prices represented approximately £896,812 in potential returns or cancellations.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of seasonal demand, high revenue products, domestic market concentration, and customer spending differences. Businesses could use these insights to support inventory planning, marketing strategies, and customer retention efforts.

Limitations¶

The dataset covers approximately one year and comes from a single retailer, limiting how broadly the findings can be generalized. Some customer IDs are missing, and certain transaction records contain unusual values. Additionally, revenue does not measure profitability because product costs are unavailable. The estimated impact of returns and cancellations should also be interpreted cautiously.
